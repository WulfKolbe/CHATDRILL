## 11.3. Geometric Invariant Theory

 

According to Felix Klein, invariant theory plays a fundamental role for ge ometry. Namely, a polynomial in the coordinates of a space is invariant  under the group of interest if and only if that polynomial expresses a geo metric property. For instance, consider the space {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0317||FO}} of binary cubics {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0029||FO}} in  Example 11.10. The hypersurface defined by {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0029||FO}} in {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO1128||FO}} consists of three points.  The vanishing of the invariant {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO2331||FO}} means that these three points are not all  distinct. 

In geometric invariant theory, one considers the variety {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO2332||FO}} defined  by all homogeneous invariants of positive degree. This variety is known as  the nullcone. Its points are known as unstable points. For a finite group {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO1161||FO}},  the nullcone consists just of the origin, {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO2333||FO}}. For {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO2308||FO}}  the situation is more interesting, and the geometry of the nullcone is very  important for understanding the invariant ring {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO2232||FO}}. Corollary 11.8 says,  more or less, that computing {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO2232||FO}} is equivalent to finding polynomial  equations that define the nullcone.
