In general, the choice of monomial order can make a huge difference in  the complexity of the reduced Gröbner basis, even for two input polynomials. 

Example 1.19 (Intersecting two quartic surfaces in projective 3 -space {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0201||FO}} ).  A random homogeneous polynomial of degree four in {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0147||FO}} variables has 35  monomials. Consider the ideal {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0038||FO}} generated by two such random polynomials.  If {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0132||FO}} is the degree reverse lexicographic order then the reduced Gröbner basis  {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0154||FO}} contains 5 elements of degree up to 7 . If {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0132||FO}} is the lexicographic order then  {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0154||FO}} contains 150 elements of degree up to 73 . 

Naturally, one uses a computer to find the 150 polynomials above. Many  computer algebra systems offer an implementation of Buchberger's algo rithm for Gröbner bases. We reiterate: our readers are strongly encouraged  to experiment with a computer algebra system while studying this book.
