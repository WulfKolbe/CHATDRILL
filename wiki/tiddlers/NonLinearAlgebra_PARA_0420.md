We have already discussed the distinction between complex rank and  real rank. A further refinement of the latter is the notion of nonnegative  rank. This very important in applications, e.g. in statistics, where one deals  with probabilities. A tensor {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO1816||FO}} is called nonnegative if its entries  {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO1817||FO}} are all nonnegative. A nonnegative rank of a nonnegative tensor {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO1567||FO}}  is the minimal number {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO0243||FO}} of nonnegative rank one tensors that sum up to {{NonLinearAlgebra_FO1567||FO}}.  In generally, nonnegative rank is larger than real rank, even for matrices.
